# Treatment v3 — "THE DAWNWORKS"

Logline: In a hand-made paper town where the sun is a lantern on a mechanical arm, the old tin robot who cranks it up every morning is secretly holding the dawn back to save a nest caught in his gears — until a girl climbs up to help, and for the first time in 41,275 mornings he sits down and watches the sunrise.

Emotional spine: a worker who has made 41,275 sunrises and never watched one. He is late because he is kind. He has never asked anyone for help. The film ends when he lets someone in and turns around to the window.

World rules (established in the first 5 s, played sincerely): night sky = black card with pinholes and light behind; clouds = cotton on threads; townsfolk = painted two-colour paper flats sliding in slots, flipping to a "day face" at sunrise; the sun = lantern on a crane arm; the sky drop rolls up on a dowel. Characters on twos, camera on ones.

Beats (96 s / 2304 frames @24):
1. 0:00-0:06 ECU of a pinhole star, light bleeding through paper fibres; pull back/crane down: pinhole sky, cotton clouds on threads, the paper town at night. The tower clock reads 6:12.
2. 0:06-0:12 Two inserts only: a paper rooster opens its beak and produces a tiny squeak; a man taps his pocket watch, holds it to his ear, looks up.
3. 0:12-0:20 Attic: Wren at the round window. Beside her a wall of small chalk suns, one per day; today's square is empty. On the hill the sun-lantern is stuck as a trembling sliver below the paper horizon; a metal groan. She grabs her lantern. Insert: her lantern dot climbing the hill path; flats turn their heads as she passes.
4. 0:20-0:30 Dawnworks interior. Hob, back to the big round window (he always faces the machine), gripping the winch handle, trembling, braced against the weight of the sun; steam puffs from his joints; thousands of scratched tally marks on the walls; chest odometer 41 275. He leans an eye-lamp to the gear housing, listens — a faint chirp — and re-tightens his grip, holding perfectly still.
5. 0:30-0:36 A trapdoor lifts: Wren's lantern, then her face, breath fogging. Hob startles but cannot let go. She thinks he needs a push, grabs the handle and yanks — his huge hand slams over hers. Freeze. Silence. CHIRP.
6. 0:36-0:48 POV from inside the gear housing: two faces peering in. Wedged between two teeth: a paper nest, three eggs, a furious mother bird. It's too dark; his hands are pinned, so with a ka-CHUNK Hob telescopes one eye-lamp out on an accordion stalk to light the gap. The bird pecks the lens: "ping" — he flinches — "pong" (these two notes become the dawn melody). Wren rolls up her sleeve, slides her small hand between the teeth, lifts the nest out... looks for a safe flat place... and sets it on Hob's flat head. His eyes roll up to look at it. The bird settles.
7. 0:48-0:56 Hob turns back to crank alone, pauses. On the other end of the winch: a second handle, dusty, cobwebbed — unused for 41,275 mornings. He wipes it with his thumb. Looks at her. She takes it with both hands. He nods the count — one, two — pull. The ratchet clicks lock to the music.
8. 0:56-1:12 DAWN as active stagecraft: counterweights drop on cords; the pinhole drop rolls up onto its dowel revealing a painted dawn; the sun-lantern swings up over the horizon on its arm; its light sweeps the town like a sundial, flats flip to their day faces, street lamps click off in a cascade, the rooster finally crows, the watch man laughs, paper birds lift; the light reaches Wren's empty window last. Clock 6:26.
9. 1:12-1:26 Tower flooded with warm light, dust in the beam. Odometer rolls to 41 276. Hob lifts his nail to scratch his mark — sees Wren drawing a small chalk sun beside his last scratch. He lowers the nail. She sits on the window beam and pats the place beside her. He glances back at the machine (habit), hesitates, sits — and for the first time turns his face to the window. Sunlight on tin; his eye-lamps soften.
10. 1:26-1:36 Slow pull-back: the Dawnworks on the hill, two small figures in the window, the bird on his head; he sits very, very still. Text: "See you tomorrow." A printed card lowers on threads: "THE DAWNWORKS — every frame and every sound written in code."
